<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="General_Summary">General Summary</span></h2>
<p>The Wonderland of the Enigma is a universe consisting of Directors and Actors. The Directors create vivid lands for Actors to perform in, called Stages. These Stages have scenes that can be manipulated by both Directors and Actors. Typically, each Act involves at least three Directors, who work together on different aspects, such as character creation, lore, and storytelling, using their combined efforts to build the Act.
</p><p>A Taste of the World &nbsp;
</p><p>Worlding Build within W.T.E
</p><p>Section-I 
</p><p>Page-I 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="WorldBuilding">WorldBuilding</span></h3>
<p>When world-building as a Director, it is essential to create a storyboard. The characters you and your Co-Directors create must be documented. When creating scenes, provide players with options for actions using Cards (discussed later). As a Director, you set the rules for these actions. It’s important to make this process your own and avoid leading players down a fixed path. World building should be fun and exciting for all Directors.
</p>
<pre>Section-I
</pre>
<pre>Page-I
</pre>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="StoryBoarding">StoryBoarding</span></h2>
<p>Storyboarding is a simple process and should not be seen as complicated. There are many ways to storyboard and set up Stages for the Actors. A storyboard can be created before or during the Acts, depending on how you wish to construct the world. Start by creating a cast of characters who will partake in the Stage you have made. There are various kinds of casts, and it’s up to you to determine the type of cast that will be within your storyboard.
</p>

<p>Wonderland of the Enigma Bounded is the standard version of the game. It locks a certain number of races and archetypes and is meant for players who want a balanced and calm gameplay experience.

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Character_Creation_aspects">Character Creation aspects</span></h2>
<p>Character creation begins with the basics: <a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Race_List">race</a>, <a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Character_Alignment's">religion</a>, <a class="mw-redirect" data-wte-link="wte://rules/Factions">faction</a>, and <a class="mw-redirect" data-wte-link="wte://rules/Job's">job</a>. Each aspect is important for personal progression. Race influences your progression, while jobs guide overall progression. Factions can slightly or completely change how you play. For example, choosing Nigraldi affects how you are perceived by the world due to its characteristics.
</p><p><a class="mw-redirect" data-wte-link="wte://rules/Archetype_Skill_List">Archetypes</a>, <a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Sub_Archetypes_Skills">sub-archetypes</a>, and <a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Classes">classes</a> add more depth. An archetype functions like a class but represents a playstyle. For example, the Prisoner archetype involves chaining, binding, and banishing, with abilities that trigger specific effects during combat. Archetypes also grant access to specific tools like automatons or voodoo dolls.
</p><p>Sub-archetypes offer additional customization, providing direct abilities. For instance, Pandora's Box allows you to turn anything into an undying soldier that regenerates over time.
</p><p>Classes are sets of learned skills acquired over time. You don’t start with a class but gain one through in-game experiences. Directors can create moments for class acquisition, such as finding a glowing sword with ancient energy during a crucial battle, indicating a new class opportunity.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Character_Skill_Points_Breakdown"><a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Leveling_up/_Reputation">Character Skill Points Breakdown</a></span></h2>
<p>So when leveling a Player or creature will gain Skill points over the duration of their leveling Process
</p><p>Character Skill Points Breakdown
</p><p>Reputation
</p><p>Reputation serves as the main leveling system in Wonder of the Enigma. Players and creatures gain reputation through various actions, including killing certain enemies, completing NPC quests, and conversing with NPCs. Reputation not only reflects a character's standing and influence in the world but also drives their progression, unlocking various benefits and abilities as they gain levels.
</p><p>Benefits and Unlocks for Reputation
</p><p>Reputation levels are tied to significant milestones in character development. As a character gains levels, they unlock various features, including new skills, abilities, classes, and racial traits. Here's a detailed breakdown:
</p>
<ul><li>Level 1: At the base level, characters unlock their full archetype, a sub-archetype skill, body compositions, and a trait. This is the foundational level where characters establish their core abilities and attributes.</li>
<li>Every 5 Levels: Characters unlock new sub-archetype levels and Wonder Point levels. Wonder Points are a special resource that can be allocated to enhance various abilities and stats.</li>
<li>Every 10 Levels: In addition to the above, characters unlock class levels and racial levels. At these points, they also have the opportunity to choose between gaining a Talent or an attribute increase. Talents provide unique abilities and enhancements, while attribute increases boost core stats like strength, endurance, charisma, dexterity, and action priority.</li>
<li>Every 15 Levels: Characters gain new Talents and further increase their Wonder Point levels, continuing to enhance their capabilities.</li>
<li>Level 25: At this milestone, characters unlock a second class, allowing for more diverse skill sets and abilities.</li>
<li>Level 50 and Beyond: Significant milestones at higher levels include unlocking racial classes, additional archetype levels, and further opportunities for Talents or attribute increases. By level 150, characters will have three different category sub-archetype levels, a new archetype class, and significant enhancements to their racial and Wonder Point levels.</li></ul>
<p>Mastery Path
</p><p>At level 125, characters can begin a Mastery Path, choosing a specific path that provides unique== Specialized Abilities and Their Modifiers ==
Specialized abilities provide unique enhancements to your character's capabilities. Each ability scales with specific stats and modifiers to ensure balanced growth and utility.
</p>
<ul><li>Level 130: Mastery Path Ability 1</li>
<li>Level 135: Mastery Path Ability 2</li>
<li>Level 140: Mastery Path Ability 3</li>
<li>Level 145: Mastery Path Ultimate Ability</li></ul>

<p>Leveling
</p><p>Characters gain levels by increasing their Reputation. Key points include:
</p>
<ul><li>Sub-Archetype Levels: Gained every 5 levels up to level 60. These points allow players to unlock and allocate abilities within their sub-archetype branch.</li>
<li>Archetype Levels: Characters gain 3 levels at level 100, allowing them to allocate these points within their archetype branch.</li>
<li>Wonder Points (WP) Levels: Characters gain +1 WP Level every 5 levels, which can be used to enhance various abilities and stats.</li>
<li>Racial Points: These points are allocated to class level, racial level, and racial archetypes, further enhancing the character's racial traits and abilities.</li></ul>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Talents_Factions_and_Ability_scores_Breakdown"><a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Talents">Talents</a> Factions and Ability scores Breakdown</span></h3>
<p>Talents, Factions, and Ability Scores
</p><p>Along with the previously discussed elements, we have Talents, Factions, and Ability Scores. Let's break down each one for a mutual understanding.
</p><p>Talents
</p><p>A creature is allotted a Talent every 10th level. Alternatively, you can choose to add 5 points to an Ability Score of your choice.
</p><p>Ability Scores
</p><p>The Ability Scores are as follows:
</p>
<ul><li>Strength: Increases strength-based rolls.</li>
<li>Endurance: Directly increases Defensive Hit Points, which will be discussed further. It deals with endurance-based rolls, which your Director will rule on.</li>
<li>Charisma: Affects charisma-based rolls.</li>
<li>Dexterity: Influences dexterity-based actions and rolls.</li>
<li>Action Priority: Scales your Action Priority skill, determining if you go first in an action, whether in combat or not.</li></ul>
<p>Defensive Hit Points
</p><p>Defensive Hit Points determine how much base damage can be mitigated. However, there are ways around Defensive Hit Points, such as Vulnerabilities and True Damage.
</p><p>Movement
</p><p>Movement determines how far you can go. However, moving over half your movement distance will cause you to become winded, forcing you to skip your next turn.
</p><p>Factions
</p><p>Factions offer your character more in-depth customization, primarily in terms of lore. Factions require certain activities, such as bounty hunting for the Deviants. This entangles your character deeper into the world, providing more opportunities for Directors to create engaging and immersive experiences.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Specialized_Ability_Scores">Specialized Ability Scores</span></h3>
<p>In addition to core stats, specialized ability scores provide more detailed and nuanced capabilities. These scores can enhance specific skills and actions your character can perform.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Specialized_Ability_Scores_2">Specialized Ability Scores</span></h4>
<ul><li><b>Inspiration:</b> Boosts creative and morale-based actions.</li>
<li><b>Versatility:</b> Enhances adaptability and the ability to perform various tasks.</li>
<li><b>Breath Control:</b> Important for underwater actions or activities requiring sustained effort.</li>
<li><b>Precision:</b> Improves accuracy and effectiveness in actions requiring fine control.</li>
<li><b>Balance:</b> Affects stability and the ability to navigate challenging environments.</li>
<li><b>Weight:</b> Impacts movement speed and physical actions, tying into endurance and attack power.</li>
<li><b>Weapon Mastery:</b> Reflects proficiency with weapons, increasing damage and effectiveness.</li>
<li><b>Perception:</b> Enhances awareness and the ability to notice subtle details.</li>
<li><b>Adaptation:</b> Increases the ability to adjust to changing situations and environments.</li>
<li><b>Mental Fortitude:</b> Boosts resilience against mental and emotional challenges.</li>
<li><b>Control:</b> Improves the ability to manage complex actions and maintain focus.</li>
<li><b>Cunning:</b> Enhances strategic thinking and deceptive actions.</li>
<li><b>Priority:</b> Ties into action priority, affecting the order of actions in combat.</li></ul>

<h2><span id="Action_Points.2FPriority"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Points/Priority"><a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Action_Priority">Action Points/Priority</a></span></h2>
<p>Action Points and Priorities
</p><p>Diving into the most important aspect of W.T.E, we have Action Points (AP) and Priorities. These two elements are crucial for the flow and strategy of gameplay.
</p><p>Action Points (AP)
</p>
<ul><li>Gaining Action Points: You gain a certain number of Action Points based on your Action Priority Ability Score, along with your specialized Priority Ability Score.</li>
<li>Using Action Points: Action Points are a resource used in various ways, such as gaining more actions during your turn, which leads to Fatigue buildup. They are also used as Class Skill resource points. When you use a class skill, it drains a specific amount of Action Points.</li>
<li>Regenerating Action Points: Action Points do not recharge passively. To regenerate Action Points, you must use downtime. The amount of Action Points regenerated during downtime is determined by the Director.</li></ul>
<p>Priorities
</p>
<ul><li>Priority Rolls: Priority is a specialized stat used for abilities. You roll your Priority to determine if your ability goes first or not in a given action, whether in combat or other scenarios.</li>
<li>Fatigue Buildup: Using additional actions through Action Points leads to Fatigue buildup. Fatigue impacts your performance and action efficiency.</li>
<li>Action Priority: This is the determining factor for the order of actions in a turn, influenced by your Action Priority skill. It ensures a dynamic and strategic flow in both combat and non-combat situations.</li>
<li class="mw-empty-elt"></li></ul>
<p>Sacrificial Actions:
</p>
<ul><li>Sacrificial Actions allow a player to forfeit their own action to grant a significant advantage to another character or to disrupt an opponent's plans. This could involve providing a bonus to an ally’s next action or imposing a penalty on an enemy's next roll.</li></ul>
<p>Counter Actions: 
</p>
<ul><li>Players can now use AP to set up Counter Actions, preparing to react to specific enemy actions during their turn. This introduces a layer of prediction and proactive defense into combat strategies.</li></ul>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="General_Rules">General Rules</span></h2>
<p>Null Archetype Restrictions: The Null archetype cannot be used in conjunction with any class, sub-archetype, or other archetype that uses magic. This is a strict rule and must be adhered to, as mixing these elements is forbidden.
</p><p>Priority Based Rolls: Priority-based rolls are integral to the game mechanics. There is no way to circumvent these rolls unless specifically allowed by the abilities themselves. This ensures fairness and consistency in gameplay.
</p><p>Pseudo Skills: Abilities considered pseudo skills will directly scale with your reputation. Players can create up to 7 pseudo skills. Creative liberties in performing actions involving multiple outlets are encouraged, provided they do not involve direct skills created by the player.
</p><p>Ability Scores: 
</p>
<ul><li>Base Ability Scores: The limit for a player's base ability scores is 20.</li>
<li>Additives: The maximum allowable increase through additives is +50.</li>
<li>Base Ability Score Additions: The maximum allowable increase through base ability score additions is 35.</li>
<li>Specialized Ability Scores: The limit for specialized ability scores is 50.</li></ul>
<p>Talent Choices: Every time you get a Talent, you can choose to receive an Attribute increase instead of selecting a Talent.
</p><p>Additional Rules and Mechanics
</p><p>Rule of Three: When creating or performing complex actions or abilities, use the "Rule of Three." This means you can combine up to three different elements (e.g., skills, items, environmental factors) to achieve a desired outcome. This encourages creativity while maintaining balance.
</p><p>Cooldown Mechanic: Certain powerful abilities or actions may require a cooldown period before they can be used again. This cooldown period is determined by the Director and can vary based on the ability's impact and power level.
</p><p>Environmental Interactions: Players are encouraged to interact with the environment creatively. Environmental factors can influence outcomes and provide strategic advantages or disadvantages. The Director will adjudicate these interactions to ensure fairness.
</p><p>Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment: To keep the game challenging and engaging, the Director can adjust the difficulty of encounters and scenarios dynamically. This ensures that players are consistently challenged and can adapt their strategies.
</p><p>Collaborative Storytelling: Players and Directors are encouraged to engage in collaborative storytelling. This means players can suggest narrative elements or outcomes, which the Director can incorporate into the game world. This collaborative approach enriches the gameplay experience.
</p><p>Actor Tokens: Introduce an Actor Tokens system, where players can earn and spend Actor Tokens to influence outcomes, re-roll dice, or gain temporary bonuses. Actor Tokens can be awarded for exceptional role-playing, creative problem-solving, or achieving significant milestones.
</p><p>Moral and Ethical Decisions: Incorporate moral and ethical dilemmas into the gameplay. Players may face situations where they must make tough choices that can impact their reputation, alliances, and the game world. These decisions should have meaningful consequences.
</p><p>Hidden Traits and Abilities: Characters can possess hidden traits or abilities that are revealed under specific conditions. These hidden elements add depth to character development and can be discovered through role-playing and exploration.
</p><p>Legacy System: Characters can leave a legacy that affects the game world even after they retire or are no longer active. This legacy can include founded organizations, discovered secrets, or lasting changes to the game world, providing a sense of continuity and impact.
</p><p>Time-Based Challenges: Introduce time-based challenges where players must complete tasks or make decisions within a set time frame. This adds urgency and tension to certain scenarios, making them more thrilling and dynamic.
</p><p>Downtime System
</p><p>Downtime in W.T.E serves as the game's equivalent to short rests, allowing players to recover and strategize. Here's a detailed breakdown of how the downtime system works:
</p>
<ul><li>Duration: 10 minutes in-game.</li>
<li>Recovery: Players recover a specific amount of health, mana, or other resources. This can be a percentage of their total or a fixed amount based on their level or abilities.</li>
<li>Actions: During downtime, players can perform minor actions such as using healing items, reloading weapons, preparing spells, or discussing strategies.</li>
<li>Interruption: Downtime can be interrupted by enemy actions or environmental hazards, depending on the Director's discretion.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Clocks_System"><a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Clocks&amp;Timetypes">Clocks System</a></span></h2>
<p>The clocks system tracks time in a way that influences both combat and world events. It divides time into active and passive periods, each affecting the game differently.
</p><p>Active Time (Combat):
</p>
<ul><li>Tracking: The Director uses a clock or timer to track the passage of time in rounds or seconds.</li>
<li>Effects: Each round of combat or intense activity advances the clock by a specific amount, influencing the world around the players.</li>
<li>Dynamic Events: As the clock progresses, predetermined events can trigger, such as environmental changes, enemy reinforcements, or critical mission objectives becoming available or threatened.</li></ul>
<p>Passive Time (World Changes and Decisions):
</p>
<ul><li>Tracking: The Director moves the clock forward based on player actions and decisions. For example, traveling between locations, performing rituals, or engaging in extended dialogue.</li>
<li>World Events: As passive time progresses, the world evolves. NPCs might move, political events could unfold, or weather patterns might change.</li>
<li>Decision Impact: Certain player actions can significantly advance the passive time clock, leading to major world changes.</li></ul>
<p>Specific Action Timers:
</p><p>Incorporating specific actions with their own time requirements can add depth and realism to the game. Below are examples of specific actions and their time requirements:
</p>
<ul><li>Reload Bow and Arrow: 1 round</li>
<li>Drink Potion: 1 round</li>
<li>Use an Action: Varies (1-3 rounds)</li>
<li>Search Area: 5 minutes</li>
<li>Disarm Trap: 2 rounds</li>
<li>Lockpicking: 1-3 minutes</li>
<li>Repair Equipment: 10 minutes</li>
<li>Set Up Camp: 30 minutes</li></ul>
<p>Integrating the Systems
</p><p>Combining downtime and clocks creates a dynamic and responsive world in W.T.E. 
</p>
<ol><li>Action Timing: Every player action advances the clock. Simple actions might advance it by a minute, while complex ones could take longer. The Director tracks this and adjusts the world accordingly.</li>
<li>Event Triggers: The Director has a list of events tied to specific times on the clock. When the clock reaches these times, the corresponding events occur. This keeps the game world active and responsive.</li>
<li>Tactical Decisions: Players must consider the passage of time when planning their actions. They might need to balance the benefits of downtime against the risks of advancing the clock and triggering new events.</li>
<li>Narrative Flow: The clocks system ensures that the narrative flows naturally, with time-sensitive events unfolding in a way that feels organic and immersive.</li>
<li class="mw-empty-elt"></li></ol>
<p>Advantage/Disadvantage System
</p>
<ul><li>Advantage: Roll two dice and take the higher result.</li>
<li>Disadvantage: Roll two dice and take the lower result.</li>
<li>Usage: Advantage and Disadvantage can be applied based on situational factors, abilities, or Director discretion.</li></ul>
<p>Critical Success and Failure 
</p>
<ul><li>Critical Success: Rolling the highest possible number on a die results in a significant positive outcome.</li>
<li>Critical Failure: Rolling the lowest possible number on a die results in a significant negative outcome.</li>
<li>Effects: The Director can determine additional effects for critical successes and failures, such as bonus damage, unexpected discoveries, or severe setbacks.</li></ul>
<p>Conditions
</p>
<ul><li>Definition: Temporary states that affect characters, such as Stunned, Poisoned, or Paralyzed.</li>
<li>Mechanics: Conditions have specific effects on actions, abilities, and overall character performance.</li>
<li>Duration: Conditions last until cured or a set time period ends.</li></ul>
<p>Exhaustion
</p>
<ul><li>Levels: Characters accumulate levels of exhaustion, each imposing greater penalties.</li>
<li>Penalties: Penalties range from disadvantage on rolls to reduced movement speed and ability usage.</li>
<li>Recovery: Exhaustion levels can be reduced through rest, abilities, or specific items.</li></ul>
<p>Morale Checks
</p>
<ul><li>Trigger: Situations that challenge a character's resolve, such as overwhelming odds or witnessing a fearsome event.</li>
<li>Mechanics: Roll against a morale score to determine if the character maintains their composure.</li>
<li>Consequences: Failure can result in fleeing, freezing, or other fear-based actions.</li></ul>
<p>Skill Challenges
</p>
<ul><li>Definition: Extended tasks requiring multiple successful skill checks.</li>
<li>Structure: A set number of successes before a set number of failures determines the outcome.</li>
<li>Example: Climbing a treacherous cliff, negotiating a complex treaty, or disarming a series of traps.</li></ul>
<p>Actor Tokens (Formerly Bennies/Fate Points)
</p>
<ul><li>Usage: Can be spent to reroll dice, negate damage, or influence the narrative.</li>
<li>Gaining Tokens: Earned through notable achievements, roleplaying, or Director discretion.</li></ul>
<p>Alignment System
</p>
<ul><li>Definition: Characters align with certain moral or ethical perspectives.</li>
<li>Impact: Affects interactions with NPCs, story outcomes, and availability of certain abilities.</li>
<li>Tracking: Alignment can shift based on actions and decisions.</li></ul>
<p>Sanity/Madness
</p>
<ul><li>Sanity Points: Track mental stability.</li>
<li>Madness Effects: Low sanity can lead to temporary or permanent madness, with specific detrimental effects.</li>
<li>Recovery: Sanity can be restored through rest, therapy, or specific abilities.</li></ul>
<p>Hit Locations
</p>
<ul><li>Mechanics: Attacks can target specific body parts, with unique effects based on the hit location.</li>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Combat_Overview"><a data-wte-link="wte://rules/Action_Types">Combat Overview</a></span></h2>
<p>1. Initiating Combat
</p><p>Combat typically begins when the players or the Director determines that a conflict is unavoidable. This can be due to an ambush, a direct confrontation, or a planned attack.
</p><p>2. Determining Action Priority
</p>
<ul><li>Action Priority Rolls: At the start of combat, each player rolls a die (typically a d20) and adds their Action Priority Ability Score to the roll. The Director does the same for each enemy.</li>
<li>Establishing Turn Order: The results of these rolls determine the turn order for the combat encounter. Higher rolls act first, creating a dynamic flow where quick and strategic thinking is rewarded.</li></ul>
<p>3. Action Points (AP) and Actions
</p>
<ul><li>Action Points (AP): Each player has a pool of Action Points determined by their Action Priority Ability Score and any specialized Priority Abilities.</li>
<li>Basic Actions: On their turn, a player can use a Basic Action, such as attacking, casting a spell, or using an item. This typically costs 1 AP.</li>
<li>Additional Actions: Players can use additional actions during their turn by spending more AP. These actions can range from making another attack to performing a special ability.
<ul><li>Fatigue: Using additional actions increases the player's Fatigue Points. For each additional action, add 3 Fatigue Points.</li>
<li>Fatigue Effects: High Fatigue Points can lower your Priority Rolls and Action Priority, impacting your effectiveness in combat.</li></ul></li></ul>
<p>4. Movement and Positioning
</p>
<ul><li>Standard Move: Move up to your base movement speed in a single turn. If your move is over half your movement speed, you will be Winded, meaning you won't be able to make another action (unless stated otherwise by the Director or a House rule).</li>
<li>Dash: Move double your base movement speed, but at the cost of using your action for the turn.</li>
<li>Cautious Move: Move half your base movement speed to avoid detection or to navigate tricky terrain without penalties.</li>
<li>Climb: Move up to half your base movement speed when climbing. Requires a Dexterity Roll if the surface is difficult.</li>
<li>Swim: Move up to half your base movement speed when swimming. Can require a Dexterity Roll in rough water.</li>
<li>Tactical Positioning: Positioning is crucial in combat. Players should consider cover, line of sight, and distance to enemies when moving. Some abilities or attacks may have range limitations or area-of-effect considerations.</li></ul>
<p>5. Attacking and Defending
</p>
<ul><li>Making an Attack: When attacking, a player rolls a die (typically a d20) and adds their relevant Ability Score (Strength for melee attacks, Dexterity for ranged attacks). The result is compared to the target's Defense.</li>
<li>Defense: A target's Defense is a combination of their Dexterity and any armor or defensive bonuses. If the attack roll equals or exceeds the target's Defense, the attack hits.</li>
<li>Damage and Defensive Hit Points: If an attack hits, the attacker rolls for damage. The target's Defensive Hit Points mitigate base damage, but some attacks may deal True Damage, bypassing Defensive Hit Points.</li></ul>
<p>6. Special Actions and Abilities 
</p>
<ul><li>Class Skills: Each class has unique skills that cost AP to use. These can range from powerful attacks to supportive abilities and require strategic use.</li>
<li>Counter Actions: Players can set up Counter Actions by spending AP, allowing them to react to specific enemy actions during their turn.</li>
<li>Sacrificial Actions: Players can forfeit their own actions to grant significant advantages to allies or disrupt enemy plans.</li></ul>
<p>7. Conditions and Effects
</p>
<ul><li>Applying Conditions: Some attacks or abilities can apply conditions (e.g., Stunned, Poisoned) to targets. Conditions have specific effects that hinder the target's performance in combat.</li>
<li>Resolving Conditions: Conditions last until cured or their duration ends. Players can use abilities or items to remove conditions.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="8._Ending_Combat">8. Ending Combat</span></h2>
<p>Combat ends when all enemies are defeated, all players are incapacitated, or another condition for ending combat is met (such as fleeing or negotiating a truce).
</p><p>Example Combat Scenario
</p><p>1. Initiating Combat: The party encounters a group of bandits. The Director calls for Action Priority Rolls.
</p><p>2. Determining Action Priority:
</p>
<ul><li>Player 1 rolls a 15 (Action Priority +3), total 18.</li>
<li>Player 2 rolls a 12 (Action Priority +4), total 16.</li>
<li>Bandit Leader rolls a 14 (Action Priority +2), total 16.</li>
<li>Bandit Minions roll a 10 (Action Priority +1), total 11.</li></ul>
<p>Turn Order: Player 1, Bandit Leader, Player 2, Bandit Minions.
</p><p>3. Player 1's Turn: 
</p>
<ul><li>Player 1 uses a Basic Action to attack a Bandit Minion, rolling a d20 + Strength (14) against the Bandit's Defense (12). The attack hits.</li>
<li>Player 1 rolls for damage, dealing 8 points. The Bandit Minion has 2 Defensive Hit Points, taking 6 damage.</li>
<li>Player 1 spends 2 AP to use an additional action, casting a spell. This adds 3 Fatigue Points.</li></ul>
<p>4. Bandit Leader's Turn: 
</p>
<ul><li>The Bandit Leader moves within range and attacks Player 2.</li>
<li>The attack hits, but Player 2 has Defensive Hit Points that mitigate some of the damage.</li></ul>
<p>5. Player 2's Turn:
</p>
<ul><li>Player 2 uses a Counter Action they prepared earlier, reacting to the Bandit Leader's attack by striking back, spending 3 AP.</li>
<li>Player 2 then uses their main action to heal themselves with a potion.</li></ul>
<p>6. Bandit Minions' Turn:
</p>
<ul><li>The remaining Bandit Minions position themselves and attack Player 1.</li></ul>
<p>7. Combat Continues: Players and enemies take turns based on the established order until the combat ends.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Directors_Role_and_Purpose">The Directors Role and Purpose</span></h2>
<p>The Directors are the storytellers and world-builders of "Wonderland of the Enigma" (W.T.E). They control the narrative, the world, and the NPCs, guiding players through a magical journey filled with endless possibilities. Whether it's fighting dragons, exploring dungeons, or traveling among the stars, the Directors create and navigate the world for the players. They can follow the canonical lore or create entirely new adventures, utilizing an assortment of Director Scene Cards to enhance the storytelling experience.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Types_of_Directors">Types of Directors</span></h2>
<p>In "Wonderland of the Enigma," Directors are essential for shaping and guiding the narrative. Each Director has specific roles and unique abilities that allow them to influence the storyline, create engaging scenes, and manage the flow of the game. Here's a detailed explanation of the different kinds of Directors and their roles:
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Primary_Director">Primary Director</span></h3>
<p><b>Role:</b> The Primary Director is the main storyteller and narrative architect. They create the overarching plot, set the scenes, and guide the general flow of the game.
</p><p><b>Responsibilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Narrative Design:</b> Develops the main storyline, including key events, plot twists, and character arcs.</li>
<li><b>Scene Setup:</b> Establishes the environment and context for each scene, describing settings, NPCs, and objectives.</li>
<li><b>Story Documentation:</b> Keeps detailed records of the narrative, tracking character actions, story progression, and potential future developments.</li></ul>
<p><b>Abilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Flashbacks (1d4):</b> Can initiate flashbacks that reveal important backstory elements or character motivations. These flashbacks can change the current scenario by providing new context or altering the outcomes of past actions.</li>
<li><b>Plot Shifts (1d6):</b> Has the authority to make significant changes to the storyline, such as introducing new threats, allies, or plot twists. This can drastically alter the direction of the narrative.</li>
<li><b>World Events (1d8):</b> Can trigger major world events that impact the entire game world, influencing the actions and decisions of the characters.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Secondary_Director">Secondary Director</span></h3>
<p><b>Role:</b> The Secondary Director supports the Primary Director by focusing on specific aspects of the game, such as managing NPCs, handling combat, or facilitating complex interactions.
</p><p><b>Responsibilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>NPC Management:</b> Controls the actions and responses of non-player characters, ensuring they behave consistently with the narrative and their motivations.</li>
<li><b>Combat and Challenges:</b> Oversees combat scenarios and other challenges, setting up encounters, determining enemy actions, and managing combat mechanics.</li>
<li><b>Detailed Interactions:</b> Facilitates complex interactions between characters, such as negotiations, puzzles, or environmental manipulations.</li></ul>
<p><b>Abilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>NPC Introduction (1d6):</b> Can introduce new NPCs into the game, providing fresh dynamics and interactions for the characters.</li>
<li><b>Environmental Changes (1d4):</b> Has the power to alter the environment, such as changing weather, terrain, or other physical elements of the scene.</li>
<li><b>Adaptive Challenges (1d8):</b> Can adjust the difficulty and nature of challenges based on character actions and narrative needs.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Antagonistic_Director">Antagonistic Director</span></h3>
<p><b>Role:</b> The Antagonistic Director oversees major antagonists and high-stakes encounters. They create and manage significant challenges that test the characters' abilities and resolve.
</p><p><b>Responsibilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Boss Encounters:</b> Designs and manages encounters with major antagonists, including powerful enemies, rival factions, or epic battles.</li>
<li><b>High-Stakes Scenarios:</b> Creates scenarios with significant consequences, such as battles that determine the fate of entire regions or critical turning points in the narrative.</li>
<li><b>Strategic Opposition:</b> Provides intelligent and strategic opposition, ensuring that major enemies behave in a challenging and believable manner.</li></ul>
<p><b>Abilities:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Epic Challenges (1d8):</b> Can create epic challenges that require careful planning, teamwork, and strategy to overcome. These encounters often serve as climactic points in the narrative.</li>
<li><b>Dynamic Opposition (1d6):</b> Has the authority to adapt the behavior and tactics of major enemies based on the characters' actions and decisions, creating a dynamic and engaging opposition.</li>
<li><b>Narrative Impact (1d8):</b> Can influence the broader narrative by determining the outcomes of high-stakes encounters. For example, a decisive victory against a major antagonist could lead to significant changes in the game world.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Flexibility_of_Director_Roles">Flexibility of Director Roles</span></h2>
<p>While the roles described above provide a structured approach to directing, it's important to note that Directors can take on multiple roles and positions if they are up to the challenge. This flexibility allows for a more dynamic and adaptive storytelling experience, where Directors can cover various aspects of the game as needed. However, the three roles outlined (Primary Director, Secondary Director, and Antagonistic Director) generally define the core responsibilities and abilities within "Wonderland of the Enigma."
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Changing_the_Narrative">Changing the Narrative</span></h2>
<p>Directors in "Wonderland of the Enigma" have the unique ability to change the narrative in significant ways, ensuring that the story remains dynamic and engaging. Here’s how Directors can influence the narrative:
</p>
<ul><li><b>Flashbacks and Side Stories:</b> By introducing flashbacks or side stories, Directors can provide new context and depth to the main narrative. These scenes can reveal hidden motivations, past events, or critical information that changes the current scenario.</li>
<li><b>Plot Shifts:</b> Directors can introduce plot shifts that alter the direction of the story. This can include revealing new threats, introducing unexpected allies, or uncovering hidden agendas.</li>
<li><b>Environmental Changes:</b> Directors can change the environment to reflect the narrative's needs. This can include altering the weather, changing the terrain, or introducing new locations.</li>
<li><b>Character Development:</b> By focusing on character development, Directors can change how characters interact with the narrative. This can lead to new alliances, rivalries, or personal quests that influence the broader story.</li>
<li><b>Alternative Timelines:</b> Exploring alternative timelines or parallel universes allows Directors to present "what if" scenarios that challenge the characters in new ways. This can lead to different versions of known events, providing fresh perspectives and challenges.</li></ul>
<p>By leveraging these tools and abilities, Directors ensure that the narrative in "Wonderland of the Enigma" remains flexible, immersive, and engaging, creating a rich and dynamic storytelling experience for all participants.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="New_Scene_Cards">New Scene Cards</span></h3>
<ul><li><b>Flashback Ap-2:</b> Introduces a flashback scene that provides background information or character development. Enhances the narrative by revealing past events that impact the current situation.</li>
<li><b>Foreshadowing Ap-2:</b> Plants a narrative seed that hints at future events or plot twists. Builds suspense and anticipation, guiding players towards key story elements.</li>
<li><b>Plot Twist Ap-3:</b> Introduces an unexpected twist in the story, altering the players' understanding of the situation. Keeps the narrative fresh and unpredictable, challenging players to adapt.</li>
<li><b>Environmental Hazard Ap-2:</b> Introduces a sudden environmental hazard, such as a storm, earthquake, or trap. Adds urgency and difficulty to the current scene, testing the players' problem-solving skills.</li>
<li><b>NPC Arrival Ap-2:</b> Introduces a new NPC to the scene, which can be an ally, enemy, or neutral character. Adds depth to the story and provides new interaction opportunities for the players.</li>
<li><b>Mystery Event Ap-3:</b> Introduces a mysterious event or clue that players must investigate. Encourages exploration and critical thinking, driving the story forward with intrigue.</li>
<li><b>Time Skip Ap-1:</b> Advances the in-game time by a significant amount, skipping over uneventful periods. Useful for moving the story along quickly without getting bogged down in minor details.</li>
<li><b>Deus Ex Machina Ap-5:</b> Introduces a powerful intervention that resolves a dire situation, often unexpectedly. Should be used sparingly, as it can drastically alter the course of the story.</li></ul>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Actors_aka_the_Player">The Actors aka the Player</span></h2>
<p>In W.T.E., players, referred to as Actors, are the driving force of the narrative, allowing the Director to weave the story. Actors play pivotal roles and can adapt to various situations, even sacrificing certain roles to keep their main character alive using stunt doubles. They can switch roles during downtime to fulfill party needs, such as becoming a healer or tanker. Actors have a variety of cards at their disposal, enhancing their ability to interact with the game world and narrative.
</p>
<h2><span id="Actor.27s_Cards"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Actor's_Cards">Actor's Cards</span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Actor_Scene_Cards">Actor Scene Cards</span></h3>
<h4><span id="Script_Chain_.28AP-1.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Script_Chain_(AP-1)">Script Chain (AP-1)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Initiates a sequential series of connected actions, allowing an actor to build upon their previous move.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants a +1 bonus to the next action roll. If the next action is a success, continue the chain for an additional +1 bonus, stacking up to a maximum of +3.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Proficiency:</b> Each level in Proficiency reduces the Action Point cost by 1 for every 3 levels (e.g., AP-1 at Proficiency 3, AP-0 at Proficiency 6).</li>
<li><b>Improvisation:</b> If Improvisation is high, allows for a re-roll of a failed action in the chain once per encounter.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Counter_.28AP-4.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Counter_(AP-4)">Counter (AP-4)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Enables an actor to counteract an opponent's action, mitigating or nullifying its effects.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Roll against the opponent's action roll. On a success, negate the action. On a critical success, reflect the action back at the opponent.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Notability:</b> High Notability can intimidate opponents, reducing their roll by the actor’s Notability modifier.</li>
<li><b>Proficiency:</b> Provides a +2 bonus to the counter roll for every 5 points in Proficiency.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Interaction_.28AP-X.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Interaction_(AP-X)">Interaction (AP-X)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Initiates a general interaction with the environment, NPCs, or other actors.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> The AP cost varies based on complexity. Simple interactions cost 1 AP, while complex negotiations or environmental manipulations can cost up to 5 AP.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 4 points in Improvisation, to a minimum of 1 AP.</li>
<li><b>Notability:</b> High Notability provides bonuses to persuasion or intimidation checks during interactions.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Counter-Interaction_.28AP-4.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Counter-Interaction_(AP-4)">Counter-Interaction (AP-4)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Allows an actor to respond to an opponent's interaction, potentially countering or altering its effects.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Similar to Counter, but specifically for interactions like negotiations or environmental changes. Roll against the opponent's interaction roll to alter the outcome.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Proficiency:</b> Provides a +1 bonus to the counter roll for each 3 points in Proficiency.</li>
<li><b>Improvisation:</b> Allows for a strategic adjustment to the counter, granting advantage on the roll once per encounter.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Past_Vision_.28AP-10.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Past_Vision_(AP-10)">Past Vision (AP-10)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Allows the actor to see a brief vision of the past, providing insights into the current situation.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants a +5 bonus to the next perception or investigation roll. Reveals hidden clues or secrets about the environment or NPCs.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Proficiency:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Proficiency.</li>
<li><b>Notability:</b> Enhances the vision, providing additional narrative insights or historical context.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Hidden_Agenda_.28AP-10.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hidden_Agenda_(AP-10)">Hidden Agenda (AP-10)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Allows the actor to set a hidden goal or objective, affecting future interactions.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants advantage on deception or persuasion rolls related to the hidden agenda. Can reveal itself as a significant plot twist.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
<li><b>Notability:</b> Provides a +2 bonus to the first roll that directly involves the hidden agenda.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Flashback_.28AP-15.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Flashback_(AP-15)">Flashback (AP-15)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Allows the actor to recall a significant memory that aids in the current situation.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants a temporary +3 bonus to a relevant stat (e.g., Strength for a physical task, Intelligence for a puzzle) for the next three rolls.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Proficiency:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Proficiency.</li>
<li><b>Notability:</b> Increases the bonus to +5 if Notability is above a certain threshold.</li></ul>

</p>

</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants advantage on physical rolls or allows the actor to avoid damage from a single attack. Can be used once per encounter.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Proficiency:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Proficiency.</li>
<li><b>Notability:</b> Allows the stunt double to perform an additional action if Notability is above a certain threshold.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Amplify_.28AP-40.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Amplify_(AP-40)">Amplify (AP-40)</span></h4>

</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
<li><b>Proficiency:</b> Increases the effectiveness bonus by an additional 10% for every 5 points in Proficiency.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Adapt_.28AP-40.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Adapt_(AP-40)">Adapt (AP-40)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Allows an actor to quickly adjust to changing circumstances, gaining a temporary advantage in the current situation.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants a +3 bonus to the next three rolls. If used in combat, also provides a temporary +2 to Defense.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
<li><b>Proficiency:</b> Increases the bonus to +5 if Proficiency is above a certain threshold.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Acceleration_.28AP-40.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Acceleration_(AP-40)">Acceleration (AP-40)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Speeds up the natural progression of events, potentially hastening the resolution of ongoing actions.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Reduces the time required for the next three actions by half. Grants a +2 bonus to initiative rolls for the next encounter.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
<li><b>Proficiency:</b> Increases the initiative bonus to +4 if Proficiency is above a certain threshold.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Momentous_Shift_.28AP-40.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Momentous_Shift_(AP-40)">Momentous Shift (AP-40)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Creates a significant shift in the narrative or the environment, altering the course of events.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Allows for a major narrative change, such as revealing a hidden plot twist or changing the environment drastically. Requires a roll, with higher rolls leading to more significant changes.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
<li><b>Proficiency:</b> Provides a +2 bonus to the narrative change roll for every 5 points in Proficiency.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Flux_.28AP-40.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Flux_(AP-40)">Flux (AP-40)</span></h4>
<p><b>Description:</b> Induces a state of flux in the environment, making it more susceptible to change and manipulation.
</p><p><b>Mechanics:</b> Grants a +3 bonus to rolls involving environmental interactions or manipulations for the next three actions.
</p><p><b>Interaction with Stats:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Improvisation:</b> Reduces AP cost by 1 for each 3 points in Improvisation.</li>
