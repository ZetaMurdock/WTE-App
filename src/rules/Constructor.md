<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Constructor">The Constructor</span></h2>
<p>Boulus Bob was a genius. He spent most of his waking years designing, refining, and perfecting machines—constructs that could think, build, and even improve upon themselves. He wasn’t content with simply creating; he sought to redefine creation itself. His breakthroughs in autonomous engineering made him a legend, but it wasn’t enough. He wanted more.
</p><p>Boulus didn’t just want to build machines—he wanted to build <i>everything</i>. Cities, worlds, ideas, even people. The very concept of construction became an obsession, and as he pushed the boundaries of what could be built, something changed. His mind no longer saw blueprints; it saw <i>frameworks of reality</i>. He understood how to assemble more than just machines—he could assemble <i>potential</i>.
</p><p>And then, one day, it clicked.
</p><p>Boulus unlocked the <b>Archetype of the Constructor</b>, becoming something more than human—something that could <i>design and manifest</i> on a level beyond comprehension. He could take raw concepts and forge them into tangible form. But what is a builder without workers? What is an Architect without a team?
</p><p>He began <b>sharing his gift</b>, granting others the ability to construct and shape their own realities, but always within his grand design. Those who accepted his knowledge became <b>Foremen</b>, able to manipulate structure, function, and form in ways that defied nature. Whether it was physical structures, intricate machines, or even the laws of existence themselves, they were all just <b>projects waiting to be built</b>.
</p><p>Boulus Bob had done it—he didn’t just build things anymore.
</p><p>He built <i>builders</i>.
</p>
