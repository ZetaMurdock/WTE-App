<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Businessman">The Businessman</span></h2>
<p>Fangilian Reed was not just a businessman—he was <i>the</i> businessman. A man whose rise through the corporate hierarchy was nothing short of legendary. From the lowest cubicle in VailTechnologies to the iron throne of its CEO, Reed’s ascent was not marked by mere hard work, but by an uncanny instinct—an eye for deals, an eye for opportunity, and an almost supernatural ability to manipulate the flow of wealth. Some called him a genius; others, just impossibly lucky. But what no one could deny was his talent for <i>control</i>—of markets, of people, of outcomes.
</p><p>Reed was not content with the mortal limitations of flesh. Money was power, but time was the true currency, and he would not be bound by its constraints. He gathered the brightest minds, the most secretive engineers, and the most radical theorists, all to construct a form that would allow him to persist indefinitely. And so he did. Now, he sits as an <i>immortal</i> executive, untouchable, unremovable, a fixture of corporate eternity. With his transcendence came something else—something more than mere immortality. His very existence became a <i>concept</i>, an <i>Archetype</i> unto itself. He was no longer just Fangilian Reed; he was <b>Reed the Businessman</b>, a living, breathing embodiment of <i>profit</i>, <i>manipulation</i>, and <i>success</i>.
</p><p>But power is meant to be <i>leveraged</i>. What is a businessman without investments? Reed developed a way to <b>franchise</b> his Archetype, bestowing its essence upon others in exchange for loyalty, service, or a contract written in terms no one could ever hope to escape. Those who signed away their names became <i>Subsidiaries</i>—walking extensions of Reed’s influence, gaining a portion of his insight, his uncanny luck, his ability to seize opportunities before others even recognized them. Each Subsidiary carried a fraction of his power, bound to the ironclad clauses of a metaphysical deal only he could fully understand.
</p><p>But the true question remains: <i>Did Reed create this Archetype himself… or was he simply the first to embody it?</i> Is he the origin, or just another product of a higher, unseen force—the eternal principle of business itself? And if so, does that mean there’s always a way to <b>buy in</b>… or to <b>hostilely take over?</b>
</p>
