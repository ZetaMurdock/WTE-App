# NPC Statblock Guide

<div class="mw-content-ltr mw-parser-output" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div style="background:#0d0d0d;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;padding:20px 24px;margin-bottom:16px;font-family:monospace;font-size:10px;letter-spacing:3px;color:#4e7fa5">
<p>WTE · DIRECTOR REFERENCE · NPC STATBLOCK GUIDE
</p>
</div><div style="background:#121212;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;border-left:3px solid #4e7fa5;padding:20px 24px;margin-bottom:16px"><div style="color:#dedede;font-size:11.5px;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:12px">NPCs in Wonderland of the Enigma are not built using the full Stalker character creation system. They use an abstracted statblock designed for Director speed without sacrificing tactical texture. This page covers how to read an NPC statblock, how to build one, how to scale one, and provides worked examples across all four NPC ranks.</div><div style="color:#565656;font-size:10.5px;line-height:1.7">NPCs run on the same mechanical rules as Stalkers in combat. They use the same four combat stats (OFF / DEF / SPD / WIL) and the same resolution systems. The difference is that Director-controlled NPCs have pre-packaged ability sets rather than the full depth of Incept, Varna, and Genus trees.</div></div><div style="background:#121212;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;padding:20px 24px;margin-bottom:16px"><div style="font-size:9.5px;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:2px;color:#4e7fa5;font-weight:700;border-bottom:1px solid #1e1e1e;padding-bottom:8px;margin-bottom:16px">NPC ROLES</div><div style="color:#909090;font-size:11px;line-height:1.7;margin-bottom:14px">Every NPC has a Role that describes their function in the world. Role determines what they do when encountered and what abilities they are likely to have. An NPC's Role is the first thing a Director should decide — it shapes everything else.</div>

<tr>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#dedede;font-weight:700">Ambient
</td>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#909090">World-filler. Not designed for combat. Merchants, bystanders, background figures. Minimal statblock — typically only HP and a social skill or two. Stats are not generated unless they become combatants.
</td></tr>
<tr>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#dedede;font-weight:700">Functional
</td>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#909090">Information or service providers. Informants, specialists, fixers. May have 1-2 non-combat abilities that are relevant to their function. Can fight if cornered — minimal combat stats.
</td></tr>
<tr>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#dedede;font-weight:700">Combat
</td>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#909090">Built for hostile encounters. Grunts, soldiers, armed operatives. Full 4-stat block. 1-4 abilities depending on Rank. These are the NPCs Stalkers fight.
</td></tr>
<tr>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#dedede;font-weight:700">Specialist
</td>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#909090">Has a specific mechanical niche: a sniper, a hacker, a medic, a summoner. Built around 1-2 defining abilities that make them function differently from a generic Combat NPC.
</td></tr>
<tr>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#dedede;font-weight:700">Boss
</td>
<td style="padding:8px 12px;color:#909090">Encounter centerpiece. Named, unique, intended to be memorable. Full statblock, 4-6 abilities, at least one Signature ability that only activates under specific conditions.
</td></tr></tbody></table>

These are guidelines — deviation for specific NPC concepts is encouraged. Bosses at any rank should have HP at the high end of their range.</div>

<div style="background:#0d0d0d;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;padding:12px 16px;margin-top:14px"><div style="font-size:9px;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;color:#565656;font-weight:700;margin-bottom:8px">QUICK HP FORMULA</div><div style="color:#909090;font-size:11px;line-height:1.7">For Combat NPCs: <b>HP = average primary combat stat × rank multiplier</b>

</p>

</td>

</td>

</td>
