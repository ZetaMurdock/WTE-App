</div><div style="background:#121212;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;border-left:4px solid #c09a30;padding:18px 22px;margin-bottom:24px;"><div style="font-size:9.5px;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:2px;color:#c09a30;font-weight:700;border-bottom:1px solid #1e1e1e;padding-bottom:8px;margin-bottom:14px;">WHAT IS THE PRESSURE ENGINE</div>The <b>Pressure Engine (PE)</b> is WTE's core resolution system. It does not set a fixed difficulty target. Instead, it measures the <b>current state of a situation</b> — how calm, volatile, or catastrophic the scene is — and tracks how that state changes as characters act.
<p>PE is a dynamic value, not a static threshold. Every action you take shifts it. The system rewards creative pressure management over raw stat accumulation.

This reflects narrative context — a calm infiltration begins low; an ambush mid-firefight begins high. The PE is <b>never hidden from players</b>.

A minimum of 1 and maximum of 4 skills may be brought to bear. More skills = higher Complexity Bonus but more dice to manage.

Failure raises it. Stalemates hold.</div><div style="margin-bottom:6px;">› <b>Manipulating</b> PE is a valid strategy — buy time, reduce tension</div><div style="margin-bottom:6px;">› <b>Surviving</b> PE means functioning while the world is on fire</div><div style="margin-bottom:6px;">› Creative multi-skill approaches generate Complexity Bonuses</div><div>› The goal is <b>narrative impact</b>, not just a higher number</div></div></div></div><div style="background:#121212;border:1px solid #1e1e1e;border-left:4px solid #a03535;padding:14px 18px;margin-bottom:24px;"><div style="font-size:9.5px;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:2px;color:#a03535;font-weight:700;margin-bottom:10px;">COMBAT &amp; ENVIRONMENTAL INTEGRATION</div>A <b>single PE value governs all systems simultaneously</b> — combat rounds, environmental hazards, and social confrontations all feed the same tracker. The Pressure Engine does not distinguish between scene types; it only distinguishes between <b>success that lowers tension</b> and <b>failure that raises it</b>. This means a combat fumble can make a negotiation harder, and a successful social maneuver can reduce the lethality of the firefight that follows.
