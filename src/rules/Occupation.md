<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Actor-Occupational_Duality">Actor-Occupational Duality</span></h2>
<p>The Occupation System in <i>Wonderland of the Enigma</i> is a hybrid progression system that integrates both <b>in-world character abilities</b> and <b>meta-narrative Actor abilities</b>, allowing players to engage in deep strategic decision-making.
</p><p>Each <b>Actor</b> (Player) has both a <b>Character Occupation</b> and <b>Actor Abilities</b> that influence gameplay at different levels
</p>
<ul><li><b>Character Occupation:</b> Determines in-world abilities, combat prowess, profession-based skills, and narrative progression.</li>
<li><b>Actor Abilities:</b> Govern meta-mechanics like role-switching, narrative control, and interaction with the Director.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Core_System_Components">Core System Components</span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Occupation_System">Occupation System</span></h3>
<blockquote><p>Each Actor (Player) has a <b>Primary Character Occupation</b> and access to <b>Fields</b> that define their skills, role, and potential mastery in the world.</p></blockquote>
<h4><span id="Character_Occupation_.28Core_Identity.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Character_Occupation_(Core_Identity)"><b>Character Occupation (Core Identity)</b></span></h4>
<ul><li>Each Actor selects <b>one</b> <b>Primary Occupation</b>, representing their fundamental role and abilities.</li>
<li>Determines <b>combat capabilities, crafting skills, professional abilities, and narrative influence.</b></li>
<li>Example: Choosing <b>Hunter</b> gives access to tracking, survival, and beast-slaying techniques.</li></ul>
<h4><span id="Fields_.28Specialization_.26_Depth.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Fields_(Specialization_&amp;_Depth)"><b>Fields (Specialization &amp; Depth)</b></span></h4>
<ul><li>Every Occupation branches into <b>Fields</b>, representing focused expertise and specialization.</li>
<li>Players can <b>commit</b> to a Field for greater depth but at the cost of flexibility.</li>
<li>Example: A <b>Hunter</b> can specialize in:
<ul><li><b>Beast Stalker</b> (Tracking and capturing dangerous creatures)</li>
<li><b>Trophy Slayer</b> (Killing rare and legendary beasts for powerful materials)</li>
<li><b>Survivalist</b> (Self-sufficiency, enhanced environmental adaptation)</li></ul></li></ul>
<h4><span id="Secondary_Occupation_.28Adaptive_Mastery.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Secondary_Occupation_(Adaptive_Mastery)"><b>Secondary Occupation (Adaptive Mastery)</b></span></h4>
<ul><li>Players may <b>temporarily switch</b> to a <b>Secondary Occupation</b> for versatility.</li>
<li>However, deep <b>Field progression is locked</b> in the Secondary Occupation—preventing full mastery.</li>
<li>Example: A <b>Hunter</b> picking up <b>Healer</b> can only access basic healing Fields like <b>Herbal Remedies</b> but cannot reach the powerful <b>Spiritual Restoration</b> Field without fully switching to Healer.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Actor_System">Actor System</span></h2>
<p>Beyond in-world Abilities, every Actor has Meta Mechanics through actor abilities which influence gameplay outside the in-game world
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Actor_Abilities">Actor Abilities</span></h3>
<ul><li><b>Unlike Occupations, Actor Abilities govern how the Actor interacts with the game’s mechanics.</b></li>
<li>Allows players to manipulate <b>scene transitions, role-switching, interaction with the Director, and strategic choices.</b></li>
<li>Example Actor Abilities:
<ul><li><b>Role Shift</b> – Allows limited swapping between <b>Secondary Occupation</b> and <b>Primary Occupation</b> at a cost.</li>
<li><b>Director’s Favor</b> – Can subtly influence NPC actions, bending the narrative in minor ways.</li>
<li><b>Scene Rewind</b> – Once per session, slightly alter the outcome of a past decision.</li>
<li><b>Foreshadowing</b> – Gain an omen or premonition about future dangers.</li></ul></li></ul>
<h2><span id="Progression_.26_Commitment_Mechanics"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Progression_&amp;_Commitment_Mechanics">Progression &amp; Commitment Mechanics</span></h2>
<p>Players advance in their Occupations and Fields through <b>gameplay actions, training, and experience points.</b> However, certain restrictions create <b>strategic depth</b>:
</p>
<ol><li><b>Full Commitment Cost:</b>
<ul><li>Players can <b>switch</b> their Primary Occupation but must <b>abandon all progress in their current Fields</b> to fully embrace a new one.</li>
<li>Commitment to a <b>Secondary Occupation</b> limits <b>depth of progression</b> in its Fields.</li></ul></li>
<li><b>Role Adaptation Costs:</b>
<ul><li>Using <b>Actor Abilities</b> to shift roles or occupations comes at a <b>meta-resource cost</b> (e.g., Exhaustion, Narrative Currency, or Time Clocks).</li></ul></li>
<li><b>Field Mastery vs. Versatility:</b>
<ul><li>Deeply investing in <b>one Occupation and Field</b> grants <b>exclusive, powerful abilities.</b></li>
<li>Balancing multiple <b>Fields across two Occupations</b> grants versatility but limits peak performance.</li></ul></li></ol>
<h2><span id="Example_Breakdown_.28Hunter_-.3E_Secondary_Healer.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Example_Breakdown_(Hunter_-&gt;_Secondary_Healer)"><b>Example Breakdown (Hunter -&gt; Secondary Healer)</b></span></h2>
<p><b>Primary Occupation: Hunter</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Fields: <b>Beast Stalker</b> (Tracking &amp; Capturing), <b>Survivalist</b> (Environmental Mastery)</li></ul>
<p><b>Secondary Occupation: Healer</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Limited Fields: <b>Herbal Remedies</b> (Basic Healing), but NOT <b>Spiritual Restoration</b> (Advanced Healing)</li></ul>
<p><b>Actor Abilities Used:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>Role Shift</b> (Switched to Healer for emergency support)</li>
<li><b>Foreshadowing</b> (Received an omen about a predator nearby)</li></ul>
