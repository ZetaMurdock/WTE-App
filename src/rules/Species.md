<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Inherence:_Species_Aspect"><b>Inherence: Species Aspect</b></span></h2>
<p>The Species Aspect of Inherence defines the biological structure, genetic behavior, and physical reality of a character.
</p><p>It governs what your body is, how it expresses genetics, and which traits are more likely to manifest over time.
</p><p>This system does not dictate personality, culture, or destiny.
</p><p>It only determines biological probability.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Template_Species"><b>Template Species</b></span></h3>
<p>Every character begins by selecting a Template Species.
</p><p>The Template Species represents:
</p>
<ul><li>The character’s primary biological form</li>
<li>Their default anatomy, proportions, and scale</li>
<li>The species they most visibly resemble</li></ul>
<p>This species acts as the genetic anchor of the character.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mechanical_Role_of_the_Template_Species"><b>Mechanical Role of the Template Species</b></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Template_Species:">The Template Species:</span></h3>
<ul><li>Establishes baseline Dominance / Recessiveness weighting</li>
<li>Determines which physical traits are easiest to express</li>
<li>Defines the default Incept Pool origin</li>
<li>Serves as the reference species when resolving genetic conflicts</li></ul>
<p>The Template Species is not “stronger” than others—it is simply more stable.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Species_Genetic_Aspects"><b>Species Genetic Aspects</b></span></h2>
<p>Each species is defined by three core genetic aspects:
</p>
<h4><span id="1._Dominance_.28D.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="1._Dominance_(D)"><b>1. Dominance (D)</b></span></h4>
<p>Dominance represents how aggressively a species’ traits assert themselves.
</p><p>High Dominance traits:
</p>
<ul><li>Express more frequently</li>
<li>Override weaker traits</li>
<li>Appear earlier in development</li>
<li>Resist suppression</li></ul>
<p>Low Dominance traits:
</p>
<ul><li>Require reinforcement or stress to appear</li>
<li>Are more likely to be altered or muted</li></ul>
<h4><span id="2._Recessiveness_.28R.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="2._Recessiveness_(R)"><b>2. Recessiveness (R)</b></span></h4>
<p>Recessiveness represents how deeply traits remain hidden.
</p><p>High Recessiveness traits:
</p>
<ul><li>Can skip generations</li>
<li>Remain dormant for long periods</li>
<li>Are more likely to emerge later via stress or mutation</li></ul>
<p>Low Recessiveness traits:
</p>
<ul><li>Are visible early</li>
<li>Are difficult to suppress</li>
<li>Rarely reappear once overridden</li></ul>
<p>Dominance controls force.
</p><p>Recessiveness controls persistence.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="3._Incept_Pool"><b>3. Incept Pool</b></span></h4>
<p>Each species has an associated Incept Pool.
</p><p>This pool represents:
</p>
<ul><li>Dormant biological capabilities</li>
<li>Latent mutations</li>
<li>Conditional traits that require pressure to activate</li></ul>
<p>Incepts:
</p>
<ul><li>Are not guaranteed</li>
<li>May never appear</li>
<li>Are tied directly to species genetics</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Genetic_Profiles"><b>Genetic Profiles</b></span></h2>
<p>A character’s Genetic Profile is the combined weighting of all species present in their Inherence.
</p><p>Each Genetic Trait:
</p>
<ul><li>Has a Dominance value</li>
<li>Has a Recessiveness value</li>
<li>Competes against other traits during expression</li></ul>
<p>The Genetic Profile determines:
</p>
<ul><li>Physical appearance</li>
<li>Structural biology</li>
<li>Trait manifestation priority</li></ul>
<p><b>Species Contribution &amp; Lineage Layers</b>
</p><p>Inherence allows for multiple species influences, layered by generation.
</p><p><b>Generation Structure</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Template Species: Genetic baseline</li>
<li>Primary Lineage: Parent species</li>
<li>Secondary Lineage: Grandparent species (optional)</li></ul>
<p>Each added species modifies the Genetic Profile.
</p><p><b>Dominance / Recessiveness Modifiers (Streamlined)</b>
</p><p>To keep lineage readable and consistent, species contributions use a flat modifier system.
</p><p><b>Base Modifiers</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Each additional species adds +2 Dominance</li>
<li>Each additional species adds –1 Recessiveness</li></ul>
<p>This reflects genetic dilution and competition.

<p><b>Species Interaction Rules</b>
</p><p>Not all species interact equally.
</p><p>When species genetics overlap, one of the following interactions occurs:
</p><p><b>1. Reinforcement</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Similar traits increase Dominance</li>
<li>Traits stabilize and express earlier</li></ul>
<p><b>2. Suppression</b>
</p>
<ul><li>One trait overrides another</li>
<li>Suppressed traits increase Recessiveness instead of disappearing</li></ul>
<p><b>3. Divergence</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Traits partially express</li>
<li>Hybrid or altered features emerge</li></ul>
<p><b>4. Instability</b>
</p>
<ul><li>Conflicting biology creates volatile traits</li>
<li>Increased likelihood of mutation or Incept activation</li></ul>
<p>Species compatibility matters as much as raw values.
</p><p><b>Trait Resolution Priority</b>
</p><p>When determining which traits manifest:
</p>
<ol><li>Higher Dominance resolves first</li>
<li>Higher Recessiveness persists longer</li>
<li>Template Species breaks ties</li>
<li>Director resolves edge cases</li></ol>
<p><b>Physical Alteration Scope</b>
</p><p>The Species Aspect of Inherence governs major physical traits, including:
</p>
<ul><li>Body size and mass</li>
<li>Limb structure</li>
<li>Sensory organs</li>
<li>Internal biology</li>
<li>External morphology</li></ul>
<p>Minor cosmetic changes are freeform.
</p><p>Major alterations must align with Genetic Profile weighting.
</p><p><b>Design Intent</b>
</p><p>This system ensures:
</p>
<ul><li>Biology is weighted, not fixed</li>
<li>Lineage matters without locking outcomes</li>
<li>Hybrid characters feel organic</li>
<li>Evolution begins at character creation</li></ul>
<p>You do not choose your traits.
</p><p>You choose your genetic odds.
</p>
