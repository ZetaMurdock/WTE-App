# Freelancer

<div class="mw-content-ltr mw-parser-output" lang="en" dir="ltr"><p><b>Contextual Authorization System</b>
</p><p><b>(Abilities Are Real — Permission Is Not)</b>
</p><p>In Wonderland of the Enigma, Inquisitors always possess their full suite of abilities. Nothing is “unlocked” in the traditional sense.
</p><p>However, the world only permits certain expressions of those abilities in certain contexts, based on your Designation and the Area Classification you are operating within.
</p><p>Power is not gained.
</p><p>Authorization is granted — temporarily.
</p><p><b>1. Area Classifications (Where You Are Matters)</b>
</p><p>Every location, system, or situation has a Classification Tier that defines how tightly reality is controlled there.
</p><p>Examples:
</p>

<p>Each zone defines:
</p>
<ul><li>What types of abilities are permitted</li>
<li>How much deviation is tolerated</li>
<li>What happens if you violate authorization</li></ul>
<p><b>2. Designation as Permission Clearance</b>
</p><p>Your Designation Rank does not give you new powers.
</p><p>It determines which contexts recognize you as “authorized.”
</p><p>Think of it as a reality firewall.
</p><p>Example:
</p>

<p>If you act within authorized zones:
</p>
<ul><li>Abilities function normally</li>
<li>No additional cost</li>
<li>No system retaliation</li></ul>
<p>If you act outside authorization:
</p>
<ul><li>The ability still works</li>
<li>But the system notices</li></ul>
<p><b>3. Unauthorized Expression (The Fun Part)</b>
</p><p>When you use an ability in a zone above your Designation, you trigger Deviation.
</p><p>You choose how far you push.
</p><p><b>Deviation Levels</b>
</p>
<table class="fandom-table">
<tbody><tr>
<td><b>Deviation</b>
</td>
<td><b>Effect</b>
</td></tr>
<tr>

</td>
<td>Ability works with noise, glitches, or reduced efficiency
</td></tr>
<tr>
<td>Major
</td>
<td>Full effect, but system backlash occurs
</td></tr>
<tr>

</td>

</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Backlash examples:
</p>
<ul><li>Reputation loss or gain (depending on outcome)</li>
<li>Temporary system flags</li>
<li>Environmental retaliation</li>
<li>Ability mutation</li>
<li>Narrative consequences (audits, trackers, attention)</li></ul>
<p>You are never told “no.”
</p><p>You are asked, “How much are you willing to pay?”
</p><p><b>4. Ability Tagging (So This Is Playable)</b>
</p><p>Abilities are tagged by Expression Type, not by power level.
</p><p>Examples:
</p>
<ul><li>Physical</li>
<li>Cognitive</li>
<li>Energetic</li>
<li>Contractual</li>
<li>Ontological</li>
<li>Systemic</li></ul>
<p>Zones restrict types, not specific abilities.
</p><p>Example:
</p>
<ul><li>Managed Zone: Physical + Cognitive allowed</li>
<li>Controlled Zone: Physical only unless authorized</li>
<li>Sealed Zone: No Ontological or Systemic without clearance</li></ul>
<p>This keeps the system flexible and fast at the table.
</p><p><b>5. Designation Doesn’t Expand Power — It Reduces Cost</b>
</p><p>This is the key philosophical move.
</p><p>Higher Designation:
</p>
<ul><li>Reduces Deviation severity</li>
<li>Converts backlash into manageable consequences</li>
<li>Allows you to “bend” zones instead of breaking them</li></ul>
<p>Low Designation characters can do anything…
</p><p>…but every action risks unraveling them.
</p><p>High Designation characters operate smoothly —
</p><p>…but are constantly watched.
</p><p><b>6. Identity &amp; Theme Payoff</b>
</p><p>This reinforces your core themes perfectly:
</p><p><b>• Identity</b>
</p><p>You are strongest where the world recognizes you.
</p><p>Elsewhere, you become an error.
</p><p><b>• Trust</b>
</p><p>Party members may have different Designations, meaning:
</p>
<ul><li>One can act freely</li>
<li>Another risks collapse for the same action</li></ul>
<p><b>• Wonder Through Control</b>
</p><p>Players experience awe not just from scale —
</p><p>but from realizing reality itself has access rules.
</p><p><b>7. Example in Play</b>
</p><p>The Inquisitor attempts a reality-folding technique inside a Controlled Zone.
</p><p>Their Designation only authorizes Physical actions here.
</p><p>Director:
</p><p>“You can do it — but it will register as a Major Deviation.
</p><p>The system will mark you. Do you proceed?”
</p><p>Player:
</p><p>“…Yes.”
</p><p>The ability fires.
</p><p>The world shudders.
</p><p>Somewhere, something writes your name down.
</p>
