# Action Priority

<div class="mw-content-ltr mw-parser-output" lang="en" dir="ltr"><hr>


<h1><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_System">Action Priority System</span></h1>
<p>Action Priority is the <b>primary temporal resolution system</b> in <i>Wonderland of the Enigma</i>.
</p><p>It replaces traditional initiative, reaction timing, and speed checks with a <b>single unified value</b> that determines <b>who acts first, who interrupts, and who controls momentum</b> in a scene.
</p><p>Action Priority is not a turn tracker — it is a <b>pressure and dominance mechanic</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="What_Action_Priority_Represents">What Action Priority Represents</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority represents an Inquisitor’s ability to:
</p>
<ul><li>Perceive threat</li>
<li>React under pressure</li>
<li>Exploit openings</li>
<li>Maintain momentum</li>
<li>Act decisively in constrained time</li></ul>
<p>It is a measure of <b>combat awareness</b>, <b>physical responsiveness</b>, and <b>decision speed</b>, not raw movement speed.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_Is_Always_Relevant">Action Priority Is Always Relevant</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority is always active, even outside of combat.
</p><p>If timing matters, Action Priority matters.
</p><p>Examples:
</p>
<ul><li>Who draws first</li>
<li>Who interrupts dialogue</li>
<li>Who reacts to a sudden hazard</li>
<li>Who moves before a trap triggers</li>
<li>Who capitalizes on a mistake</li></ul>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_as_a_Stat">Action Priority as a Stat</span></h2>
<p>Every Inquisitor and creature has an <b>Action Priority value</b> listed in their stat block.
</p><p>This value may be modified by:
</p>
<ul><li>Fatigue</li>
<li>Conditions</li>
<li>Injuries</li>
<li>Gear</li>
<li>Environment</li>
<li>Active abilities</li></ul>
<p>If no roll is called for, the <b>static Action Priority</b> is used as the default resolution.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="When_Action_Priority_Is_Rolled">When Action Priority Is Rolled</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority is rolled <b>only when timing is contested</b>.
</p><p>Common triggers include:
</p>
<ul><li>Initial combat engagement</li>
<li>Interrupt attempts</li>
<li>Quicktime moments</li>
<li>Reaction disputes</li>
<li>Simultaneous actions</li>
<li>Ambush resolution</li></ul>
<p>If timing is not contested, no roll is required.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Initial_Engagement_Roll">Initial Engagement Roll</span></h2>
<p>At the start of combat or high-tension interaction:
</p>
<ol><li>The Director determines if a creature <b>Engages</b> or <b>Remains Stagnant</b>.</li>
<li>The Director rolls Action Priority for the opposing force.</li>
<li>One or more Inquisitors roll against it based on context.</li></ol>
<p>The result determines:
</p>
<ul><li>Who establishes momentum</li>
<li>Who acts first</li>
<li>Whether an ambush succeeds or fails</li></ul>
<p>This roll does <b>not</b> determine the entire fight — it determines <b>the opening state</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Engaged_vs_Stagnant_States">Engaged vs Stagnant States</span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Engaged">Engaged</span></h3>
<p>Represents active readiness:
</p>
<ul><li>Advancing</li>
<li>Drawing weapons</li>
<li>Charging abilities</li>
<li>Tactical movement</li></ul>
<p>Engaged creatures are eligible for <b>immediate interaction</b> and Quicktime triggers.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Stagnant">Stagnant</span></h3>
<p>Represents delayed intent:
</p>
<ul><li>Monologues</li>
<li>Observation</li>
<li>Overconfidence</li>
<li>Waiting for a trigger</li></ul>
<p>Stagnant creatures are more vulnerable to <b>interruptions and reversals</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_During_Combat">Action Priority During Combat</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority does <b>not</b> create strict turn order.
</p><p>Instead, it governs:
</p>
<ul><li>Who interrupts whom</li>
<li>Who responds first</li>
<li>Who overwhelms in exchanges</li></ul>
<p>When two actions overlap, Action Priority is checked.
</p><p>Higher result controls the flow.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span id="Defensive_Responses_.26_Action_Priority"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Defensive_Responses_&amp;_Action_Priority">Defensive Responses &amp; Action Priority</span></h2>
<p>When targeted, an Inquisitor may attempt:
</p>
<ul><li><b>Avoid</b> → Dexterity Save</li>
<li><b>Endure</b> → Endurance Save</li>
<li><b>Parry</b> → Dexterity Save + Weapon Mastery</li></ul>
<p>Action Priority influences:
</p>
<ul><li>Whether the response happens in time</li>
<li>Whether the defense is clean or partial</li>
<li>Whether follow-up attacks are possible</li></ul>
<p>Failure does not always mean damage — it may mean <b>loss of momentum</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Overwhelm_State">Overwhelm State</span></h2>
<p>If an Inquisitor fails defensive resolution and the attacker retains Action Priority:
</p>
<ul><li>The target is <b>overwhelmed</b></li>
<li>The attacker may continue attacking if actions allow</li>
<li>This continues until interrupted or priority shifts</li></ul>
<p>Overwhelm is <b>temporary and situational</b>, not a condition.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span id="Action_Priority_.26_Fatigue"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_&amp;_Fatigue">Action Priority &amp; Fatigue</span></h2>
<p>Fatigue directly impacts Action Priority.
</p><p>Rules:
</p>
<ul><li>Each AP-based action increases Fatigue</li>
<li>Fatigue applies penalties to Action Priority rolls</li>
<li>At critical fatigue, static Action Priority is reduced</li></ul>
<p>This prevents action spam and enforces pacing.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span id="Action_Priority_.26_Conditions"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_&amp;_Conditions">Action Priority &amp; Conditions</span></h2>
<p>Certain conditions directly alter Action Priority:
</p>
<ul><li>Stunned → Priority suppressed</li>
<li>Anchored → −1 Priority</li>
<li>Prone → Priority disadvantage</li>
<li>Airborne → +1 Priority, −Balance</li></ul>
<p>Action Priority is often the <b>first stat affected</b> by control effects.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_Outside_Combat">Action Priority Outside Combat</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority also governs:
</p>
<ul><li>Interrupting speech</li>
<li>Snatching objects</li>
<li>Dodging hazards</li>
<li>Reacting to sudden environmental changes</li></ul>
<p>If two Inquisitors attempt something at the same moment, Action Priority resolves it.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority_in_Quicktime_Combat">Action Priority in Quicktime Combat</span></h2>
<p>During a Quicktime moment:
</p>
<ol><li>Director declares Quicktime.</li>
<li>All involved Inquisitors roll Action Priority.</li>
<li>The highest result determines the micro-outcome.</li></ol>
<p>Quicktime does not replace combat — it <b>compresses it</b> into a decisive moment.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Static_vs_Rolled_Priority">Static vs Rolled Priority</span></h2>
<ul><li><b>Static Priority</b> → Used when timing is uncontested</li>
<li><b>Rolled Priority</b> → Used when timing is contested</li></ul>
<p>Directors should default to <b>static</b> unless drama or outcome depends on speed.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Director_Guidance">Director Guidance</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority should:
</p>
<ul><li>Control pacing</li>
<li>Reward awareness</li>
<li>Punish hesitation</li>
<li>Create cinematic tension</li></ul>
<p>Do <b>not</b> roll it constantly.
</p><p>Roll it when the moment <i>matters</i>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Common_Misunderstandings">Common Misunderstandings</span></h2>
<ul><li>Action Priority is not initiative</li>
<li>It does not lock turn order</li>
<li>Higher Priority does not guarantee damage</li>
<li>Lower Priority does not mean helpless</li></ul>
<p>It determines <b>who controls the moment</b>, not who wins the fight.
</p>
