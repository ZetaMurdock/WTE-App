# Mechanics

<div class="mw-content-ltr mw-parser-output" lang="en" dir="ltr">

<h1><span id="Mechanics_Overview_.E2.80.93_Wonderland_of_the_Enigma_.28W.T.E.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Mechanics_Overview_–_Wonderland_of_the_Enigma_(W.T.E)">Mechanics Overview – Wonderland of the Enigma (W.T.E)</span></h1>
<p>Welcome to the <b>Mechanics</b> page.
</p><p>This page is a <b>high-level synopsis</b> of all major systems in <i>Wonderland of the Enigma</i>.
</p><p>Nothing here is meant to be exhaustive — this is an <b>overview</b>, meant to give Players and Curators a foundational understanding of <b>how the game functions</b>, how interactions resolve, and how the systems talk to each other.
</p><p>W.T.E is a <b>cinematic, dynamically-run TTRPG</b> set in a <b>Sci-Fi / Low Fantasy</b> universe.
</p><p>Yes, there is space travel.
</p><p>Yes, there is advanced technology.
</p><p>But the setting is buried under <b>laws, regulations, spatial rules, and consequences</b> that make the world feel grounded, constrained, and <i>believable</i> rather than loose or hand-waved.
</p><p>The goal of the system is:
</p>
<ul><li>Deep, intrinsic play</li>
<li>Heavy customization</li>
<li>Mechanical consequences that feel <b>thematic</b>, not arbitrary</li>
<li>Fewer “because the Curator said so” moments</li>
<li>More <i>earned</i> outcomes</li></ul>
<p>Below is a breakdown of the <b>core systems</b> and their subsidiaries.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Action_Priority">Action Priority</span></h2>
<p>Action Priority is W.T.E’s streamlined replacement for traditional initiative.
</p><p>Every Inquisitor (player or creature) has an <b>Action Priority stat</b>. This stat is used as the <b>default resolution tool</b> whenever turn order, reaction timing, or action sequencing matters — unless the Curator decides otherwise due to context.
</p><p>There are <b>two major uses</b> of Action Priority:
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="1._Initial_Engagement">1. Initial Engagement</span></h3>
<p>At the start of combat or a high-tension interaction, a creature will either:
</p>
<ul><li><b>Engage</b></li>
<li><b>Remain Stagnant</b></li></ul>
<p>Engaging doesn’t mean attacking immediately. It can be:
</p>
<ul><li>Slowly advancing</li>
<li>Drawing or unsheathing a weapon</li>
<li>Activating systems</li>
<li>Taking posture</li></ul>
<p>Remaining stagnant can be:
</p>
<ul><li>Monologuing</li>
<li>Observing</li>
<li>Charging an ability</li>
<li>Waiting deliberately</li></ul>
<p>These states can also be <b>interchanged mid-scene</b>.
</p><p>When engagement begins, the Curator contests <b>Action Priority</b>.
</p><p>The Curator rolls for the creature, then chooses which Inquisitor rolls against it based on:
</p>
<ul><li>Distance</li>
<li>Awareness</li>
<li>Context</li>
<li>Hidden variables</li></ul>
<p>This determines <b>who gains the initial edge</b>.
</p>
<h3><span id="2._Combat_Flow_.26_Contests"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="2._Combat_Flow_&amp;_Contests">2. Combat Flow &amp; Contests</span></h3>
<p>Once combat is underway, Action Priority becomes collaborative.
</p><p>When an Inquisitor is targeted, they may respond by:
</p>
<ul><li><b>Avoiding</b> → Dexterity Save</li>
<li><b>Enduring</b> → Endurance Save</li>
<li><b>Parrying</b> → Dexterity Save + Weapon Mastery Check (requires proper gear)</li></ul>
<p>Failure results in being <b>overwhelmed</b>, allowing follow-up attacks if the attacker has sufficient actions and is uninterrupted.
</p><p>This is a <b>prime mechanic</b> and the backbone of how interactions resolve.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Actions">Actions</span></h2>
<p>Actions are granted primarily through <b>Paradigms</b>, but all Inquisitors interact with the same core action framework.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Standard_Actions">Standard Actions</span></h3>
<p>Includes:
</p>
<ul><li>Striking</li>
<li>Blocking</li>
<li>Using abilities</li>
<li>Interactions (grabbing, touching, speaking, tasting, manipulating objects)</li></ul>
<p>Standard Actions may be used at the discretion of the Player and the Curator based on timing and context.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bonus_Actions">Bonus Actions</span></h3>
<p>Bonus Actions allow:
</p>
<ul><li>Striking</li>
<li>Blocking</li>
<li>Parrying</li>
<li>Certain abilities</li></ul>
<p>Parrying requires a weapon or sufficient gear.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prepared_Actions">Prepared Actions</span></h3>
<p>Prepared Actions are slower but more powerful.
</p><p>They allow abilities to execute at <b>maximum effectiveness</b>.
</p><p>Prepared Actions include:
</p>
<ul><li>Setting traps (fire, electric, concussive, combustion, etc.)</li>
<li>Charging complex abilities</li>
<li>Recuperation or short recovery</li></ul>
<p>Prepared Actions typically take <b>5–10 minutes</b>, with a hard cap around <b>20 minutes</b>, and may overlap with <b>Downtime</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span id="Opportunity_.26_True_Opportunity_Actions"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Opportunity_&amp;_True_Opportunity_Actions">Opportunity &amp; True Opportunity Actions</span></h3>
<p>These actions are triggered by <b>specific events</b>.
</p>
<ul><li><b>Opportunity Actions</b> respond to openings</li>
<li><b>True Opportunity Actions</b> are reserved for counter-specific mechanics</li></ul>
<p>Unless explicitly stated, Opportunity Actions <b>do not</b> allow free counterattacks.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span id="ITT_.E2.80.93_In-Game_Time_Tracking"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="ITT_–_In-Game_Time_Tracking">ITT – In-Game Time Tracking</span></h2>
<p>W.T.E has a built-in time system.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Passive_Time">Passive Time</span></h3>
<p>Passive Time is <b>always moving</b>.
</p><p>It tracks:
</p>
<ul><li>Missions</li>
<li>Travel</li>
<li>Event triggers</li>
<li>World reactions</li></ul>
<p>Curators may use the built-in calendar or create their own.
</p><p>Passive Time exists to:
</p>
<ul><li>Add urgency</li>
<li>Give weight to small decisions</li>
<li>Make conversations and delays matter</li></ul>
<p>It takes getting used to, but once understood, it becomes one of the most immersive systems in the game.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Active_Time">Active Time</span></h3>
<p>Active Time occurs during <b>combat and ability execution</b>.
</p><p>Every Ability and Genus (spell) has a time cost.
</p><p>Those costs accumulate and <b>advance Passive Time</b> either during or after combat.
</p><p>Combat doesn’t pause the world — it accelerates it.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Leveling">Leveling</span></h2>
<p>Leveling has <b>three interconnected layers</b>:
</p>
<ol><li><b>Level</b></li>
<li><b>Experience</b></li>
<li><b>Eminence</b></li></ol>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Level">Level</span></h3>
<p>There are <b>20 base levels</b>, with progression beyond that using percentage scaling (20.5, 21, etc.), influenced by <b>Inherence</b> (character creation).
</p><p>Levels do not hard-cap characters.
</p><p>Each level increases <b>how much information and growth</b> a character can sustain.
</p><p>Level 20 is not the end — it’s usually the beginning.
</p>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Experience">Experience</span></h3>
<p>Experience is earned through:
</p>
<ul><li>Completing quests</li>
<li>Killing monsters or abominations</li>
<li>Advancing story objectives</li></ul>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Eminence">Eminence</span></h3>
<p>Eminence ranges from <b>+20 to −20</b> and reflects:
</p>
<ul><li>Actions</li>
<li>Dialogue choices</li>
<li>Moral consistency</li></ul>
<p>Eminence modifies Experience gain through a <b>multiplier</b>:
</p>
<ul><li>Starts at <b>1.2×</b></li>
<li>Caps at <b>1.7×</b></li></ul>
<p>Consistency strengthens the multiplier.
</p><p>Inconsistency weakens it.
</p><p>Doing good while acting good increases gains.
</p><p>Doing bad while acting bad also increases gains — just on the opposite spectrum.
</p><p>Contradictory behavior causes slowdown.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Combat_Overview">Combat Overview</span></h2>
<p>Combat in W.T.E is <b>diverse, contextual, and mechanically dense</b>.
</p><p>There are multiple combat states, but the most common is <b>Anchored Combat</b>.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Anchored_Combat">Anchored Combat</span></h2>
<p>Anchored Combat represents <b>restricted movement due to context</b>.
</p><p>Example:
</p><p>Your leg is pinned under a boulder while fighting a mountain predator.
</p><p>Anchored does <b>not</b> mean helpless — it means the fight becomes <b>desperate, reactive, and cinematic</b>.
</p><p>(Full Anchored rules detailed below.)
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ranged_Attacks">Ranged Attacks</span></h2>
<p>Ranged attacks <b>always hit</b> unless:
</p>
<ul><li>The target has cover</li>
<li>The target Dodges</li>
<li>Another creature intercepts</li>
<li>Environmental effects interfere</li></ul>
<p>Ranged attacks create a <b>Quicktime Window</b> if:
</p>
<ul><li>Fired from elevation</li>
<li>Target is mid-movement</li>
<li>Projectile is visible and slow enough</li></ul>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aerial_Attacks">Aerial Attacks</span></h2>
<p>Airborne creatures gain:
</p>
<ul><li><b>+1 Action Priority</b></li>
<li><b>−1 Balance</b></li></ul>
<p>If struck mid-air:
</p>
<ul><li>Balance Check required</li>
<li>Failure causes a crash, fall, or stall</li></ul>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Melee_Attacks">Melee Attacks</span></h2>
<p>Melee is the <b>most stable</b> combat form.
</p>
<ul><li>Auto-hit unless dodged or parried</li>
<li>Entering melee range may trigger reactions</li>
<li>Parry replaces Dodge with Strength or Weapon Proficiency</li>
<li>Frequently creates Quicktime moments</li></ul>
<hr>
<h2><span id="Anchored_State_.28Expanded.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Anchored_State_(Expanded)">Anchored State (Expanded)</span></h2>
<p><b>Anchored</b> means movement is severely limited or removed entirely.
</p><p>It is never voluntary.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Causes_include:">Causes include:</span></h3>
<ul><li>Grapples</li>
<li>Restraints</li>
<li>Terrain</li>
<li>Severe wounds</li>
<li>Fatigue</li>
<li>Magical or psychic immobilization</li></ul>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Effects:">Effects:</span></h3>
<ul><li>Movement capped at 5 ft or none</li>
<li>−1 Action Priority</li>
<li>Dodge disadvantaged or impossible</li>
<li>Vulnerable to positional attacks</li></ul>
<hr>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Breaking_Free:">Breaking Free:</span></h3>
<ul><li>Spend an Action → Break Check</li>
<li>Use abilities or items</li>
<li>Ally assistance</li>
<li>Quicktime window</li></ul>
<p>Break Checks use Strength, Dexterity, or specialty vs Curator-set difficulty.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Underwater_Combat">Underwater Combat</span></h2>
<ul><li>Movement halved</li>
<li>Ranged attacks fail past 10 ft</li>
<li>Fire/lightning altered</li>
<li>Sound amplified</li>
<li>Slashing converts to bludgeoning</li></ul>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Space_Combat">Space Combat</span></h2>
<ul><li>No resistance</li>
<li>Infinite movement until halted</li>
<li>Drift vectors matter</li>
<li>Anchoring to objects is critical</li></ul>
<p>Highly narrative by design.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quicktime_Combat">Quicktime Combat</span></h2>
<p>The signature mechanic of W.T.E.
</p><p>Triggered by:
</p>
<ul><li>Vulnerable animations</li>
<li>Visible attacks</li>
<li>Clashes</li>
<li>Dodgeable projectiles</li>
<li>Cinematic tension</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Resolution:">Resolution:</span></h3>
<p>Both sides roll <b>Action Priority</b>.
</p><p>Outcome determines the micro-result.
</p>
<hr>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span></h2>
<p>Death is <b>rarely instant</b> unless:
</p>
<ul><li>True damage is involved</li>
<li>Soul or Spirit damage occurs</li>
<li>Massive environmental hazards apply</li>
<li>The Curator determines the blow was unquestionably fatal</li></ul>
